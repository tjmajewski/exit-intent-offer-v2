30-day section 5 reads 42 no-discount against 11 discount decisions, which looks

decision writes a row and returns; every discount decision crashed before

Related: `offerSuppression` shipped 09-18 (`d9071fd`) and the store installed

### 1.3 I shipped a margin-negative threshold lane and it reached production

`af9bb56` scaled the threshold reward against the **qualifying total** instead
of the spend the ask adds. On the median cart that produced *"spend $350 more,
save $300"*: the merchant earns $140 on the added spend and gives back $300,
losing $160 on a shopper who was already converting. Strictly worse than showing
nothing.

Fixed in `db3f593`, which went out alone for that reason. Recorded because the
failure mode generalises: `MAX_GAP_MULTIPLE` guards only one direction — how
large the ASK may be per dollar saved — and nothing capped the SAVING per dollar
of ask. It never bound before because the reward was always tiny, so scaling the
reward silently removed the only thing holding the deal together.

### 1.4 The wig keywords were real but irrelevant

`829b3fc` widened `beauty` to catch wigs, and I told the merchant that was why
her store read `unknown`. Wrong. The products query never got far enough to look
at a product type — see §4.

### 1.5 The evolution cron is NOT stalled, and the alarm saying so is the bug

09-21 §4 records "evolution cron stalled ~4.4 days", and I repeated it in an
earlier draft of this document and in §8. Wrong, twice over.

**The cron runs hourly and is running.** It evolves a cell only when that cell
has **100+ impressions since its last cycle**
(`app/cron/evolution-cycle.js:72`), where a cell is one `baseline × segment`
pair — 6 baselines × mobile/desktop/all. The live store has 137 impressions in
30 days across roughly 10–15 live cells, so ~10 per cell against a bar of 100.
At ~4.5 impressions a day a cell needs **months** to qualify. Declining to
evolve a population on 9 data points is the correct behaviour.

**What is broken is the health check.** `api.health.jsx` infers "evolution cron
stalled" from *no `Variant.birthDate` newer than 2 hours while traffic exists* —
a condition this store cannot satisfy, ever, at its traffic level. It has fired
continuously since install.

That is not a cosmetic problem. HANDOFF-2026-09-21 §3 lists it as one of three
mechanisms that hid the week-long discount outage: an alarm that cannot be
satisfied trains the eye to ignore the endpoint it comes from. And it cannot
distinguish "running and correctly waiting for data" from "not scheduled at
all" — identical signal — so it is a false positive now and useless as a real
tripwire later.

The fix is to alarm on the cron **running**, not on it **acting** — or to gate
the freshness check on some cell having actually crossed 100 impressions. **Not
done**; see §8.

**Second correction: the new offer amounts do not need evolution.** I wrote
that a frozen bandit cannot re-explore them. Evolution is the genetic layer —
killing losers, breeding new genes. Thompson Sampling runs on **every request**,
picking among existing live variants, and `scaleDollarOffer` is applied at serve
time to whichever gene it picks. The new amounts are live now and independent of
the evolution cycle.

## 2. The dollars-off lanes did not scale with the cart

### 2.1 What was wrong

`FIXED_DISCOUNT.offerAmounts = [5,10,15,20]` and
`THRESHOLD_DISCOUNT.offerAmounts = [10,15,20,25]` are **dollars**.
`PERCENT_DISCOUNT.offerAmounts = [10,15,20,25]` are **percent**.

On a $1,150 cart at aggression 8 that meant:

| lane | served | % of cart |
|---|---|---|
| PERCENT_DISCOUNT | 10–20% | $115–$230 |
| FIXED_DISCOUNT | $5–$16 | **0.4–1.4%** |
| THRESHOLD_DISCOUNT | +$100 → $20 | **1.7%** |

A visitor is split between the percent and fixed lanes by a **visitorId hash**
(`flatDiscountBaseline`), not by anything learned. So two statistically
identical shoppers were offered $230 and $16, and the bandit was being asked to
compare those as if the difference were copy.

The aggression dial could not move the dollar lanes either: the aggression cap
chops the gene to $16 *before* the margin ceiling — which would have allowed
$235 — is ever consulted.

### 2.2 The fix

One function, both lanes: `scaleDollarOffer(gene, basis)` in
`ai-decision.server.js`. The gene is read as a **dollar floor AND a percent of
the basis, whichever is larger**, nice-rounded.

`basis` is the **cart** for a flat offer and the **incremental spend**
(threshold − cart) for a conditional one. Not the qualifying total: a threshold
offer is funded only by the margin on the spend it adds, and sizing it against
the whole threshold sizes it against money the merchant already had. That
distinction is §1.3.

Plus `maxConditionalDiscount(cartValue, threshold, assumedGrossMargin)` — never
give back more than half the margin the added spend generates, matching the flat
lane's `shareCap`. At the default 40% it lands on exactly the bound
`MAX_GAP_MULTIPLE = 5` expresses from the other side, so the two constants now
agree by construction rather than by luck.

This is also what makes `offerCeilingPercent`'s exemption of conditional offers
from the propensity taper *true*. That exemption rests on "a threshold costs
nothing unless the basket grows", which only holds while the discount stays
small relative to the growth.

### 2.3 What the live store serves now

Cami Wigs, aggression 8, median cart $1,150:

| lane | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| FIXED_DISCOUNT | $5 · $10 · $15 · $16 | **$60 · $120 · $170 · $180** |
| THRESHOLD_DISCOUNT | +$100 → $20 | **+$175 → $35 … +$350 → $70** |
| PERCENT_DISCOUNT | unchanged | 10–20% ($115–$230) |

Every threshold arm is margin-positive; the preview prints the merchant's kept
margin per arm so that is checkable rather than asserted.

Stores at **$100 carts and below are untouched** on the fixed lane — the gene
acts as a floor, so nothing shrinks. The threshold lane is the exception:
small-cart stores now serve **smaller** threshold offers than before this series
(a $146 cart asked for $44 more funds $8, not $20), because those offers were
already margin-negative. The arms flatten there, since the gap genuinely funds
one number.

## 3. Gross margin is inferred now, never asked for

`settings.assumedGrossMargin` **has no UI and nothing in the app has ever
written it.** The ternary in `offerCeilingPercent` fell through to `0.40` for
every store on the platform, always.

That is ~15 points low for a beauty store and 15 points **high** for an
electronics one — the dangerous direction, because it authorizes discounts on
real money the margin cannot fund. A 25%-margin store was cleared to hand out
20%.

Asking the merchant is not the fix: a margin field during onboarding is a
blocker on getting a store live. Shopify's own cost-per-item is on
`InventoryItem.unitCost`, which needs a `read_inventory` scope this app does not
hold (`write_products,write_discounts,read_orders`) and which most merchants
leave blank.

So `grossMarginForShop(shop, explicit)` keys off the vertical the app already
derives from the store's own catalog. Priors are the **low** end of each
vertical's DTC range, because the error is asymmetric:

| vertical | prior | | vertical | prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| beauty | 0.65 | | sports, pets | 0.42 |
| health | 0.60 | | toys | 0.40 |
| jewelry, fashion | 0.55 | | food | 0.35 |
| home | 0.45 | | electronics | **0.25** |
| other / unknown | 0.50 | | | |

**Unknown is 0.50** by explicit product decision: a new install should not be
throttled to a low-margin ceiling before the weekly cron classifies it, and the
aggression dial is the merchant's own lever. The cost is that an unclassified
electronics store is over-authorized for up to a week. That trade is recorded in
the code comment so nobody rediscovers it as a surprise.

**This matters less than it looks for high-margin stores.** Above ~50% margin
the binding constraint stops being margin and becomes `aggrCap = 10 + 1.5 ×
aggression` (max 25%). Cami Wigs at 40% → 65% moves her flat ceiling 20% → 22%.
A generous prior cannot run away with the discount; that is asserted in
`tests/gross-margin-prior.test.js`.

The real win is the other direction — electronics from a 20% authorization to
6%.

## 4. Vertical derivation has never worked, on any store

### 4.1 Root cause

`deriveVertical` asked for `products(first: 50, sortKey: BEST_SELLING)`.
`BEST_SELLING` is **not a member of `ProductSortKeys`** — it exists only on a
collection's products connection.

Shopify answers a validation failure with **HTTP 200**, a top-level `errors`
array and a **null `data`**. The old code read straight through it to
`undefined` and returned `null` — which is the same `null` a store selling 50
unrelated things returns.

So the query failed for every store since it shipped, and looked exactly like a
correct answer. `derivedVertical` was `null` platform-wide and nobody could tell
that apart from "unclassifiable".

**This is HANDOFF-2026-09-21 §3 again**: a failure that writes nothing is
indistinguishable from a decision.

### 4.2 The fix

Dropped the `sortKey` rather than correcting it. A vertical is a majority vote
over the catalog and does not need the best sellers specifically; ranking them
is a second failure mode for no benefit.

`deriveVerticalDetailed` now reports which of six things happened —
`no_offline_session`, `http_error`, `graphql_error`, `no_products`,
`no_keyword_match`, `no_majority` — with the product types it actually saw, so
the next vocabulary gap is a one-line diagnosis instead of a guess.
`deriveVertical` stays a thin wrapper, so no caller changed.

Also widened `beauty` to `hair / wig / extension / lash / braid / weave`, with a
test that they did not swallow electronics, health, jewelry or pets.

### 4.3 What this cost the live store

Cami Wigs ran her entire trial with `derivedVertical = null`, which meant no
margin prior **and** no cross-store cluster priors.

The cluster priors were worth nothing anyway — see §7.1 — but the margin prior
was real.

## 7. Known gaps, deliberately not fixed

§7.1–§7.4 are from 09-22, §7.5–§7.8 from 09-24.

### 7.1 `aovBand` needs 5 conversions and cross-store priors need 3 stores

`aovBand` derives from the `Conversion` table with a 5-row minimum. Cami Wigs
has 2, so it stays null — even though her $1,150 median cart is trivially the
`high` band and that number is sitting in `VariantImpression`.

**Fixing it would buy nothing today.** Cluster priors read
`MetaLearningInsights` rows written by the aggregation cron, which returns early
at `shops.length < 3`. She is the only store, so there are zero insight rows and
`getBaselineCvrPrior` returns `null` regardless of her vertical or band. It
becomes real at 3+ stores, not before.

### 7.2 The percent and fixed lanes are still split by a hash, not by learning

`flatDiscountBaseline` assigns the lane by `visitorId` hash. That is now
defensible — the lanes land within 2x of each other on the same cart, asserted
in `tests/offer-scaling.test.js` — but it is still not a learned choice. The
bandit compares copy within a lane, never the lanes themselves.

### 7.3 A mint failure is still a 500

HANDOFF-2026-09-21 §6 item 2 remains the highest-leverage change here. A mint
failure should degrade to the no-discount modal plus a recorded
`failure/discount_mint_failed` suppression, not a 500 that shows nothing and
writes nothing. Not done.

### 7.4 No cell will evolve for months, and the health check lies about it

Not a bug in the cron — see §1.5. At ~4.5 impressions a day against a 100/cell
bar, the genetic layer will not run on this store in any near horizon. "The
engine learns per store" is true of Thompson Sampling at serve time and not yet
true of evolution. That is a traffic fact, not a defect, and the honest thing is
to stop calling it one.

The defect is the health check, which reports it as a stalled cron and has fired
continuously since install. Fixing that is worth more than anything about the
cron itself: an alarm nobody can satisfy is an alarm nobody reads, which is
exactly how the 09-21 outage survived a week.

### 7.5 `confirm-render` is rate-limited to 30/min for the entire platform

**FIXED in `aa674d4`, not yet deployed.** §18.1. The diagnosis below stands as
written; twelve endpoints shared the flaw rather than the five named here.
Left in place because the reasoning is the reason the tiers are sized as they
are now.

**It was not introduced on 09-24 — it predates all three of that session's
commits.**

`app/routes/apps.exit-intent.api.confirm-render.jsx:23` limits to 30 requests per
60s keyed on `${route}:${ip}`, where the IP comes from `getClientIp`
(`app/utils/rate-limit.server.js:40`). That function prefers `fly-client-ip`,
which Fly sets to whoever opened the connection — and for an app-proxy request
**that is Shopify's proxy, not the shopper.** So the bucket is not per-shopper or
even per-store. It is one global bucket, shared by every shopper on every store,
for the endpoint that confirms a modal actually rendered.

Why it matters more than it looks:

- A dropped confirm leaves `rendered=false` forever. That row then looks
  identical to a decision that genuinely never fired — which is the exact
  signal §12 was investigating and §13.2 was built to measure.
- `Variant.impressions` only moves in `confirmImpressionRender`, and
  `InterventionThreshold.showImpressions` only in `confirmInterventionRender`.
  A throttled confirm silently withholds both. Evolution fitness and adaptive
  threshold learning both quietly under-count.
- The loss is **not random**. It correlates with platform-wide traffic at that
  minute, so busy periods lose disproportionately — the opposite of what any
  reader of those counters would assume.

It has not bitten yet only because the platform has one live store at ~4.5
impressions a day. It becomes real at the second or third store.

`decision-miss` (`:54`) inherits the same flaw at 60/min and is worse exposed:
it beacons per decided page rather than per render, so it will saturate first.
Its own doc comment already says a null reason means "not recorded", which keeps
the data honest, but it will be lossy long before anyone expects.

**The fix is in `getClientIp`, not in the limiters.** An app-proxy request
carries the shop in its validated query string; those routes should bucket on
`shop` (or `shop` + the beacon's decision id) rather than on a header that
resolves to Shopify. Every public app-proxy endpoint shares this — `track-click`,
`track-variant`, `journey`, `confirm-render`, `decision-miss` — so it is one
change in one function, not five.

Worth pairing with a second look at whether these limits should exist at all on
endpoints whose payload is already bound to a server-minted decision id.

### 7.6 `Take $17% off your order` reaches real shoppers

**FIXED in `c4a0cfa`, not yet deployed.** §18.2. The cause was Guided mode
pinning a constant copy pool regardless of the pinned offer type — not gene
selection in general, which is where this section guessed it would be. The
`{{threshold_remaining}}` worry at the end was unfounded: the storefront fills
it. Details in §18.2.

Confirmed from live rows on `exit-intent-test-2`. The copy gene is
`Take {{amount}}% off your order` — a percentage template — but the decision type
is `fixed` with amount 17. Interpolation substitutes `{{amount}}` as a bare
number for `percentage` offers and as currency otherwise, so the shopper sees
`$17%`.

The console mirrors this deliberately and must keep doing so — showing exactly
what the shopper saw is the point of that column. **The fix belongs in gene
selection** (`app/utils/variant-engine.js`, `app/utils/gene-pools.js`): a
percent-worded gene must not be paired with a fixed or threshold offer, either
by tagging genes with the offer types they are valid for or by rewriting the
template for the chosen type.

Also unresolved: `{{threshold_remaining}}` appears unsubstituted in stored copy
(`You're just {{threshold_remaining}} away from $8 off`). The console's
`interpolate` only fills `{{amount}}` and `{{threshold}}`, mirroring the
storefront. Whether the storefront fills the third at render or whether it
reaches shoppers raw has not been checked.

nothing and keeps getting chosen. §12. The "Does each trigger actually fire?"
table reports it; no code reads it. Once §13.2 has data, the show rate per
trigger gene is the natural thing to fold into selection.

### 7.8 No render test of `DecisionLog`

## 12. Why modals were never firing

The question the table made askable. Traced in code, not measured — see §16.

**`exit_intent` on mobile was close to unfirable.** `mouseout` does not exist on
a touch device, so the gene fell through to a capped idle timer, and
`setupIdleTrigger` resets on `touchmove` and `scroll`. A shopper who keeps
scrolling never accumulates the stillness the timer waits for. The only mobile
visitor who could see the modal was one who stopped and stared for 15 seconds.

`app/utils/gene-pools.js:118` has claimed since it was written that `exit_intent`
means *"mouse leave (desktop) / back-button (mobile fallback)"*. **There was no
back-button handling.** No `popstate`, no `pagehide`, no `visibilitychange`
anywhere in the extension. The capped idle timer was standing in for a
capability the comment asserted existed.

Nothing penalised it, either: variants score on conversions per **rendered**
impression, so the misses never enter the denominator and the gene keeps getting
picked. The "Does each trigger actually fire?" table on the Performance tab is
still the only thing that sees this, and nothing acts on it.

Two smaller causes, both silent: the competing-popup gate drops the attempt
after ~60s, and nothing anywhere recorded why a decision died.

## 13. What shipped for it

### 3.1 Mobile can fire now — via dwell, not a guessed exit signal

`setupDwellTrigger(seconds, show, label)` fires after N seconds **on the page**
with no interaction listeners at all. This is the part that actually answers
§12: idle waits for the shopper to stop, and browsing is exactly what keeps
resetting it, so "engaged enough to be worth an offer" and "sitting perfectly
still" are near mutually exclusive on a phone. Dwell has no such contradiction.
It is a deliberate interruption rather than a guess at intent.

`setupTabReturnTrigger(show, label)` fires when the tab comes back after ≥10s
away. It deliberately does **not** show while `document.hidden` — a modal
rendered to a screen nobody is looking at is an impression that was never seen.

Both armed on the Pro path, the Enterprise path, and the pill-escalation watch,
for any gene asking for exit intent. The capped idle timer stays as a floor.

Two other signals were built and then removed before deploy — see §14.1.

### 3.2 A miss now carries a cause

New nullable `InterventionOutcome.missReason`, new public endpoint
`app/routes/apps.exit-intent.api.decision-miss.jsx`, beaconed with
`navigator.sendBeacon` on pagehide (a normal fetch is cancelled as the page
tears down). Closed vocabulary server-side so a stale client cannot write
arbitrary strings:

| Reason | Means |
|---|---|
| `left_before_dwell` | The dwell timer was still counting. |
| `left_before_idle` | The idle timer was still counting. |
| `competing_popup_dropped` | A third-party popup held the screen past ~60s. |
| `trigger_never_fired` | Triggers armed, no timer pending, nothing fired. |

Rendered as a subdued line under the Result badge, full sentence in the
expansion.

**A null reason means "not recorded", never a cause.** A dropped beacon, blocked
storage, or a storefront script cached from before this shipped all land there.
Read the counts as a lower bound per cause, not a partition of the misses. This
matters more than usual given §7.5.

Render stays terminal: `confirmInterventionRender` clears the reason when it
flips `rendered`, and the endpoint refuses to touch a row that already rendered.

## 14. What the QA pass caught

Two review agents over `10f02e4` and `2f493bc`. Nine confirmed bugs. Recording
them because the pattern — a fix that was worse than the bug, shipped on
inference with no way to measure it — is the thing to not repeat.

### 4.1 The first mobile fix would have interrupted shoppers mid-scroll

Both signals in the first attempt were removed:

- **Scroll flick.** 240px inside 300ms is **800 px/s** — an ordinary swipe, not
  a flick. iOS momentum scrolling and a theme's smooth scroll-to-top both clear
  it, and `isMobileDevice()` is `innerWidth <= 768 || /mobile/i`, so a narrow
  desktop window armed it too. It also re-fired ~9 times per gesture: the
  re-baseline condition (`travelled <= 0 || elapsed > FLICK_MS`) is false for
  every event inside the window that just fired. **Scroll velocity is not
  evidence of intent to leave.**
- **`popstate`.** Fires on *same-document* history traversal only. A real back
  gesture out of the store is cross-document: the page unloads and `popstate`
  never fires on it. So it missed every actual exit and fired on the one thing
  it must not — themes that `pushState` for collection filters (Dawn's
  `FacetFiltersForm` does), where backing out of a filter would pop a modal.

### 4.2 `showModal` had no re-entrancy guard — pre-existing

`this.modalShown` is set at the **bottom** of the method, after an `await` on
`updateModalWithAI` → a `/cart.js` fetch. The only guard was at the top. Any
trigger firing twice inside that window ran the whole show twice: two
`stampModalShown()` calls burning the 30-day frequency ceiling for one show, two
`trackVariant('impression')` calls inflating the bandit's denominator and
suppressing the variant's own measured CVR.

Desktop `mouseout` has always been able to fire repeatedly, so **this predates
today** — the scroll flick turned a latent hazard into a certainty. Now latched
with `this.showInFlight` around `showModalInner()`.

### 4.3 The miss reason was measuring platform, not cause

It was derived from "was an idle timer armed", and on mobile one always is. So
`trigger_never_fired` was unreachable on mobile and the reason was constant —
unable to answer the one question the deploy exists to ask.

Compounding it, the client latched after one send and beaconed on
`visibilitychange`, so a five-second glance at a notification wrote the generic
reason and froze it; a competing popup found forty seconds later could never be
recorded. Fixed: reads which timer is pending, no latch, re-sent only when the
reason changes, and the endpoint takes the **last** reason while the row is
unrendered.

### 4.4 Console bugs

- **A conversion implied a render.** `recordInterventionConversion` can attribute
  with `proveRender:false`, so a decision whose modal never displayed can still
  convert — and "Converted" put a success badge on a row that also carried the
  reason it was never seen. Now **"Bought anyway"**.
- **The fold kept a miss reason on a decision that rendered.** Duplicate
  `(shopId, aiDecisionId)` pairs exist in production (the schema says so, and
  says why the unique index cannot be added yet), so a rendered row could pair
  with an unrendered one and read "Shown · left before idle" on one line.
- **Every pre-decision claimed "nothing was going to be shown"** while its own
  Offer column named a discount. `determineOffer` returns no copy gene; copy is
  chosen later, when a visitor actually arrives.
- **A client could claim a server-side origin.** A crafted
  `requestReason: "cart_webhook"` rendered a live visitor's row as "no visitor
  was on the page". Client and server origins are separate namespaces now.
- **An unrecorded cart rendered `$0`.** `Number(null)` is 0, and `??` does not
  help when both writers store an explicit null.

### 4.5 Pre-existing, fixed in passing

The `InterventionOutcome` lookup had no `shopId`, so it could not use
`@@index([shopId, aiDecisionId])` — there is no standalone index on
`aiDecisionId` — and full-scanned the table on every console load.

### 4.6 Tests, 244 → 268

`tests/decision-miss.test.js`, `tests/format-timezone.test.js`.

The outcome fold moved out of the loader into `foldOutcomeRows` because it was
not testable there; the multi-row case the schema warns about had no coverage at
all. `app/utils/format.js` had zero tests despite carrying a hydration-safety
doctrine as its file header.

**Two tests written in `2f493bc` could not fail** — one asserted "a rendered row
shows no miss" while passing `missReason: null`, the other sliced on a string
literal that silently becomes "rest of file" when `indexOf` returns -1. Both

## 18. The second 09-24 session

### 18.1 App-proxy rate limits were one global bucket — `aa674d4`

§7.5 was right about the cause and understated the blast radius.

`getClientIp` prefers `fly-client-ip`, which Fly sets to whoever opened the
connection. On an app-proxy request that is **Shopify's proxy**, so
`${route}:${ip}` was not per-shopper, not per-store, but one bucket per route
shared by every shopper on every store. §7.5 named five endpoints; it is
**twelve** — every public app-proxy route. `admin.login` is the one caller
correctly left on IP, because that request reaches us directly.

The fix is `enforceProxyRateLimit`, which buckets on the `shop` Shopify puts in
the proxied query string. That `shop` is real and not a hopeful read: it is
what `shop-settings` and `custom-css-public` have always used to find the
store, so if it were absent the modal could not initialise at all. It shares
`isValidShopDomain` with those routes, so a domain they would reject cannot
claim a bucket here either.

**The numbers had to move with the key, and this is the part worth reading.**
They were platform-wide totals. Carried onto a per-shop key unchanged they
would have converted a platform bug into a per-merchant outage — 10/min on
`ai-decision` would have capped one store at ten visitors a minute. They are
named tiers now (`PROXY_LIMITS`), sized by what an endpoint costs against what
dropping it costs: telemetry most generous, because a drop there corrupts a
counter silently rather than failing loudly; minting a price rule tightest.

Known tradeoff, documented in the module: `shop` is read before the signature
is validated, so a crafted request can pick its own bucket. It still cannot do
anything — `authenticate.public.appProxy` rejects it a few lines later — and
that is a better trade than a shared bucket that *guaranteed* cross-store
throttling for the same weak evasion resistance.

**What to check after deploy.** The one assumption not yet exercised against a
real proxied request is that `shop` is where `getProxyShop` looks. If it were
not, every request would silently fall to `unattributed:<ip>` — which is the
old global bucket wearing a new name, and it would look exactly like success.
So do not read "no 429s" as confirmation. Confirm positively:

```bash
# On the container. Both are already read from the query string by name, so a
# 200 from either proves the param is present where the limiter reads it.
flyctl logs -a resparq --no-tail | grep -iE "shop-settings|custom-css-public"
```

### 18.2 `Take $17% off your order` — `c4a0cfa`

One line in the Hybrid branch. Guided mode lets a merchant pin the offer TYPE
and AMOUNT, then forced `baseline = 'conversion_with_discount'` for any

### 18.6 Negative remaining spend — what is actually true

§18.5's first write-up said the storefront renders "Add $-100 more" to
shoppers. **That was wrong, and it was wrong in the familiar direction: an
argument from one line of code, not from the path a shopper takes.** Recorded
in full because the overstated version was already in a commit message and
said out loud.

There are three copies of `Math.ceil((threshold - cartValue) / 5) * 5` in
`exit-intent-modal.js` — `:3266`, `:3348`, `:3504` — and none of them clamps at
zero. That much is true. It does not follow that a shopper sees the negative.

**The live path is safe.** `LIVE_AI_RENDER = true` in both the repo and the
asset the storefront actually serves (checked, §15.1), so threshold modals
render through `resolveModalContent`. That function computes the negative, fills
it into the copy — and then, at `:3561`, tests `cartValue >= decision.threshold`
and on a qualifying cart replaces the headline, the subhead AND the primary CTA
outright:

```
You unlocked $70 off!  /  Your cart qualifies. This discount is applied at
checkout.  /  Keep Shopping
```

Nothing carrying the negative survives that. A non-threshold decision cannot
carry one either — `decision.threshold` is null, so the ternary yields 0.

**The fallback path is not safe.** `updateModalWithAI` (`:3234`) runs when

override. Its only corrective is at `:3348`, and it fires **only when the

### 21.1 Negative remaining spend — one helper, two call sites

The defect was never really the missing `Math.max(0, …)`. It was that **one rule

## 25. A QA pass over §24, and what it found

Asked for directly: have an agent check the archetype work. It found a real bug
in code §24 had audited without noticing, two false claims in §24 itself, and
three tests out of 22 that could not fail. **Not deployed.**

The pattern is worth naming: `7bce14f` was a commit about misleading logs whose
own log claim ("unconditionally") was misleading, in a file whose plan gate was
lying about which stores it applied to. Writing the audit did not make the author
immune to the thing being audited.

### 25.1 The plan gate defaulted an unset plan to a paid tier

`ai-decision.jsx` read `shopRecord.plan || 'pro'` in **four** places (the priors
gate, `isEnterprisePlan`, the decision payload's `plan`, and a log). `Shop.plan`
defaults to `"starter"` (`schema.prisma:40`) and `plan.server.js` resolves an
unrecognized value to `"starter"`. So a row with a null or empty plan was treated
as **pro** by the storefront and **starter** by the admin UI, and had archetype
priors switched on.

Fixed with one resolver — `resolvePlanTier` exported from `plan.server.js`, used
by both it and all five call sites in the route (the fifth already defaulted to
`starter`, so it now agrees by construction rather than by coincidence). Fifth
copy of this fallback would have been the same shape as `1765f2a`'s four copies
of the threshold rule.

This also settles §23.1: priors are **not** on for every shop. Starter is off,
which is what the new `disabled_for_plan` reason reports.

### 25.2 "Unconditionally" was false on ~70% of decisions

Three paths in `selectVariantForImpression` return before the priors block:
single live variant (`:521`), the **70% champion shortcut** (`:645`), and
champion-is-only-contender (`:659`). On all three, `7bce14f` printed no priors
line at all while the caller still printed `priorsGate: pro` — the exact false
confirmation the commit existed to remove, on the majority of decisions for any
store with a champion. A champion can be promoted by hand from the admin UI, so
this does not depend on the frozen evolution cron.

Each early return now logs `[Priors] Archetype biasing NOT CONSULTED (<reason>)`
— `only_one_live_variant`, `champion_70pct_shortcut`, `champion_is_only_variant`.

### 25.3 The inactive log overstated its own scope

It said "sampling is uniform". It is not: cluster priors, per-cell segment stats
and trigger stats all still shape the Beta samples. Only the archetype
multiplier is absent. Now reads "no archetype weighting applied".

`no_segment_key` is effectively dead — `composeSegmentKey` always returns a
fully-qualified string, so in production the reason resolves to
`disabled_for_plan`. Kept as a guard against a future caller, not as a reason
anyone should expect to see.

### 25.4 Template priors: wrong cause named, and no disabled branch

`computeTemplatePriors` returns `source: 'none'` for two different reasons — no
signal at all, and `templates.size < 2` (plenty of signal, one template). The log
asserted the first. Now says "no signal or only one template with signal", and
`if (enableTemplatePriors)` has an `else` so the Enterprise-only gate says so
instead of printing nothing. The tournament line reports its skip reason too.

### 25.5 The cross-store sources boosted a lone archetype; own-shop refused to

`tryOwnShopPriors` has always returned null at `rankings.length < 2` ("need
spread to differentiate"). The meta paths called `rankingsToPriors` directly, and
its `n === 1` branch hands that archetype `MAX_BOOST` while every other stays
neutral at 1.0 — a 1.30 tilt on evidence with no comparison, in exactly the
situation the own-shop path rejects. A one-archetype insight is a normal output
of `aggregateArchetypePerformance`, so this was reachable.

One rule for all three sources now (`hasSpread`, `archetype-priors.js:88`). This
is the only **behaviour** change in the QA fixes; everything else is logging,
the plan resolver, and tests.

### 25.6 Three of the 22 tests could not fail

Found by mutation, not by reading. The two ranking tests listed the intended
winner first in the rows array, so `Map` insertion order plus a stable sort
reproduced the expected result even with `converted` **ignored entirely** or the
`rankings.sort` **deleted outright**. Both mutants passed all 22.

Fixed by listing the loser first in both, plus three new tests: conversions are
read at all (equal row counts, 0% vs 100%), three archetypes ordered by rate
from a deliberately wrong input order, and the `select` clause pinned. The
wall-clock window assertion was widened from an 864 ms budget to an hour —
anything actually wrong with a 30-day window is wrong by days.

Re-verified by mutation: ignoring `converted` now fails 4 tests, deleting the
sort fails 4, narrowing the `select` fails 1, removing the spread rule fails 2.
26 tests, full suite 358 pass.

### 25.7 Accepted, not fixed

- **The `no_data` line prints on every decision, forever.** The QA pass calls
  this structurally the same "alarm nobody can satisfy" as §1.5 and §7.4. The
  counter-argument, taken: it is a per-decision *diagnostic* line among ~5
  others, not an alarm with a threshold, and its whole purpose is to answer
  "has biasing ever fired?" without a hand-run query. Revisit if it ever drowns
  something.
- **Stale line citations across §11–§23.** `7bce14f` shifted `variant-engine.js`
  and `ai-decision.jsx`, so numbers cited in earlier sections are off by a few.
  §24's own citations are corrected; the older ones are left. Cite by symbol
  name when it matters.
- **`template-priors.js` still has no tests.** §24.6.

### 26.3 What the logs should say afterwards, and what would be wrong


- `Archetype biasing INACTIVE (source=none, no data at this segmentKey: …)`

- **Zero `[Priors]` lines at all.** Means none of these paths ran, which after

- **`Archetype biasing active`.** Would mean 50 rendered rows landed in one

### 26.5 What the deploy proved, and what it did not

**Proved:** v91 is serving, health check passes, and the storefront is reaching

**Not proved: the new logging has never been exercised.** Zero `[Priors]` lines

`ai-decision` call happened in that window. The decision endpoint fires on cart
activation, and this store produces ~4.5 of those a day. So every claim in §25.2

of §12 and §13. Same shape, same cause: no traffic.

This is the one thing to check next time there is any:

```bash
flyctl logs -a resparq --no-tail | grep '\[Priors\]'
```

`--no-tail` matters. Without it the command streams forever, and macOS has no
`timeout` to bound it — a `timeout … | grep` pipeline fails with
`command not found` and prints **nothing**, which reads exactly like "no
matching lines". That false negative happened once already while verifying this
deploy.

### 26.6 The §7.4 health check is now the only noise in the logs

Every 60 seconds, as an `Error:` with a full stack trace:

```
Error: [health] Evolution cron stalled — newest Variant.birthDate is 11713m old
(max 120m). Traffic exists (impression at 2026-09-25T18:04:38.641Z).
```

Unchanged by this deploy and not caused by it — §1.5 and §7.4 describe it, and
it has fired continuously since install. Worth noting here only because it is now
the *only* error-shaped output in a clean log, so the next person grepping for a
real problem wades through it first. It also cannot be satisfied: at ~4.5
impressions a day the evolution cron will not breed for months (§7.4), so the
alarm is permanent by construction.

That is §8 item 4, and this is the argument for moving it up: the logs are the
instrument §26.3 asks the next session to read, and there is one permanent false
error in them.
